01 / WORK SAMPLE

Contain risk without losing the evidence

Scenario: a managed laptop reports a potentially unwanted application and unusual outbound activity. This is a fictional tabletop exercise. An alert alone does not establish a breach or confirmed data loss.

Triage worksheet

QuestionEvidence / decision
What triggered the alert?Record detection name, device, time and console reference
How reliable is the signal?Correlate endpoint details and available network/user context
What could be affected?Identify device owner, business role and exposed resources
Is containment needed?Follow the approved incident procedure and severity criteria
Who owns the next step?Assign the incident lead and business communication owner

Response sequence

  1. Preserve available alert details and timestamps in the approved incident record. Limit access to personnel with a need to know.
  2. Validate the device and user context. If policy warrants, isolate the endpoint using the approved management method; coordinate a safe alternative for the user.
  3. Escalate with observed facts, confidence level, impact and actions taken. Distinguish confirmed observations from hypotheses.
  4. Investigate and remediate under the incident lead’s direction. Preserve relevant evidence before destructive cleanup or reimaging.
  5. Validate endpoint health and required access before reconnecting. Monitor for recurrence and document residual uncertainty.

User communication

“We’re investigating a security alert on your laptop and have temporarily restricted its connection. Please stop using it and avoid deleting files or installing anything. I’ll help you with the approved alternative and update you at the agreed time.”

02 / WORK SAMPLE

Close with evidence, not assumptions

The controls below illustrate how I would organise operational evidence. They do not certify compliance with any framework or represent an audit of a real organisation.

Evidence checklist

ControlEvidence exampleAcceptance question
Endpoint protectionCurrent inventory and health reportAre in-scope devices reporting as expected?
Disk encryptionEncryption status and controlled key recordIs encryption active and recovery controlled?
Mobile managementEnrolment and policy statusAre assigned devices enrolled and compliant?
AccessApproved role and membership reviewDoes access still match business need?
Incident closureTimeline, containment and validation recordIs the closure decision justified and owned?
Follow-upRemediation action with owner and due dateWill the cause or control gap be addressed?

Illustrative management update

“One device is isolated while the security team validates the alert. No confirmed data exposure has been established from the evidence reviewed so far. The user has an approved temporary work option. The next update will cover investigation findings, remediation and the reconnection decision.”

Lessons-learned prompts

  1. Which signal was useful and which remained ambiguous?
  2. Did the response preserve evidence and limit business interruption?
  3. Were escalation contacts, approvals and communication ownership clear?
  4. What policy, awareness or technical change would reduce recurrence?

Boundary of responsibility

IT supplies technical evidence and implements approved controls. Compliance, legal and business owners determine their respective obligations and decisions. This sample does not claim lead-auditor or legal authority.
